Classification v4.1 · current

Every sourced claim carries a tag inline at the start of the claim, like VND-1. The letters say what kind of source it came from. The number says how much of it you can check for yourself. No issue has published under v4.1 yet; this section describes the vocabulary the first one will use. Full versioned vocabulary files back every list on this page.

Source type

GOV

Government agency, advisory, statement, court filing, indictment

VND

Commercial security vendor research, IR reporting, blog

MED

News and trade press

ACA

Academic research, whether or not peer-reviewed

TEC

Structured record: CVE entry, KEV catalog, RFC, repository, patch note, certificate log

IND

Independent researcher, personal blog, conference talk

NPO

Non-profit and community: Shadowserver, Spamhaus, Team Cymru, ISACs, non-governmental CERTs

AGG

Automated aggregator or machine-generated summary: threat feeds, AI-written digests, auto-summarized newsletters

SOC

Social and messaging post from a non-adversary account: researcher threads, channel posts, forum discussion

ADV

Adversary-controlled: leak site, ransom note, actor claim, forum ad

ANL

This product's own judgment. Not a source

Grade

1

Direct, fully evidenced. The source saw it and showed its work. You can check the artifacts

2

Direct, partially evidenced. The source saw it but did not publish enough for you to check

3

Direct, aggregate. The source saw it in data only it holds. Nobody outside can reproduce the number

4

Relay, primary identified. The source is repeating a named finding it did not observe

5

Relay, primary unreachable. Someone is named as having found this and no document can be opened

6

Unattributed assertion. Nothing stands behind this but the assertion itself

The digit means the same thing under every prefix: it measures the claim's evidentiary posture, not the industry the publisher sits in, and it does not describe hunt value or how easily a reader can obtain the document.

Access mark

Separate from the grade. open is the default and is not printed. gated means the document was retrieved and graded but a reader may need a subscription to reach it. withdrawn means the document has since returned 404 or 410, and the archive pointer becomes the citation. unarchived means no archive provider captured it; the citation stands and the reader is told the copy is not backed up.

Analyst blocks

ANL takes no digit. Confidence is expressed as ANL-High, ANL-Moderate, or ANL-Low, capped by the weakest input the block rests on. Hunt Priority blocks, the executive summary, and the assessment and outlook section are all ANL constructs and are marked as such.

From a tag to a claim to a source

Every tag carries a data-claim attribute naming a claim identifier, and every claim block carries that identifier as its own anchor with a printing permalink. The identifier resolves at /claims/{serial}-{id}/, which redirects to the sentence where the claim was first published and lists every later issue that has cited it since. The claim's ledger entry names the source_id it rests on, and that source's full record, admission history, and live/backfill pools render at /coverage/sources/. That chain, tag to claim to source, is what "checkable" means on this site.

v1 · Iran and DPRK, published before classification v3.7

The Iran and DPRK issues predate this classification system and use a different, simpler four-category scheme, with no grade digit. This section describes what those tags meant, drawn from what those issues actually print.

AM

Ambient: widely distributed information, standard training data, general public knowledge

IX

Indexed: publicly available but requires deliberate searching, academic databases, archived materials

GT

Gated: behind access restrictions, paywalls, subscriptions, authentication requirements

SP

Specialized: requires domain expertise to interpret correctly

Both published issues also append a qualifier suffix to most of these tags, for example [AM-gov], [IX-vendor], or [SP-technical], naming the specific source type or the nature of the specialized judgment. This is an addition to the same four base categories, not a distinct version, and carries no grade digit or gate-based computation behind it.
