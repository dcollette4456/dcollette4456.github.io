## Executive Summary

Open-source reporting over the past thirty days shows continued, multi-pronged North Korean (DPRK) cyber activity pressing against South Korean government, defense-adjacent, financial, and software-supply-chain targets, with clear implications for any network that interconnects with Republic of Korea (ROK) government or U.S. Forces Korea infrastructure, including systems that feed into the Korea theater portion of the Department of Defense Information Network (DoDIN-K).

Four threads stand out:

1. A joint advisory from four South Korean security agencies formally linked the Lazarus Group to the Guntra ransomware operation, describing shared infrastructure and hosting and laying out an active wateringhole campaign against Korean websites.

2. Kimsuky continued to expand from pure espionage into higher-throughput operations, compromising a South Korean groupware vendor to reach downstream SaaS customers and, separately, becoming the first publicly documented state actor observed running a self-hosted large language model stack on its own command-and-control servers to triage stolen documents.

3. An unattributed but strongly suspected intrusion into South Korea's Ministry of Foreign Affairs diplomatic training platform went undetected for roughly nine months, underscoring persistent gaps in detection across ROK government systems.

4. Financially motivated activity continued to intersect with state operations, most visibly through Bybit's new U.S. federal lawsuit against the DPRK government, Lazarus, and the Reconnaissance General Bureau over the 2025 $1.5 billion casino heist.

Separately, this month's Ulchi FreeShield exercise (17–27 August) will for the first time formally exercise cyber and GPS-denial scenarios alongside drone warfare, reflecting a ROK–U.S. assessment that DPRK combat experience gained in Russia/Ukraine is feeding back into its cyber and electronic-warfare posture against the peninsula.

## Scope and Sourcing

This report synthesizes open-source reporting published between approximately 12 July and 11 August 2026, drawn from South Korean national security agencies (National Intelligence Service, National Police Agency, Korea Internet & Security Agency, Financial Security Institute), private threat intelligence firms (AhnLab, Genians Security Center, ENKI WhiteHat), and international OSINT feeds.
