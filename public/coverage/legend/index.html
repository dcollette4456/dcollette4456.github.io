Classification v3.7 (current)

Every sourced claim carries a tag in the left margin like VND-1. The letters say what kind of source it came from. The number says how much of it you can check for yourself.

Source type

GOVGovernment agency, advisory, statement, court filing, indictment
VNDCommercial security vendor research, IR reporting, blog
MEDNews and trade press
ACAAcademic and peer-reviewed
TECStructured record: CVE entry, KEV catalog, RFC, repository, patch note, certificate log
INDIndependent researcher, personal blog, conference talk
NPONon-profit and community: Shadowserver, Spamhaus, Team Cymru, ISACs, non-governmental CERTs
ADVAdversary-controlled: leak site, ransom note, actor claim, forum ad
ANLThis product's own judgment. Not a source

Grade

1Direct, fully evidenced. The source saw it and showed its work. You can check the artifacts
2Direct, partially evidenced. The source saw it but did not publish enough for you to check
3Direct, aggregate. The source saw it in data only it holds. Nobody outside can reproduce the number
4Relay. The source is repeating a named finding it did not observe
5Unretrievable. The evidence trail ends somewhere nobody can open

The digit means the same thing under every prefix: it measures the claim's evidentiary posture, not the industry the publisher sits in, and it does not describe hunt value or how easily a reader can obtain the document.

Access mark

Separate from the grade. open is the default and is not printed. gated means the document was retrieved and graded but a reader may need a subscription to reach it. withdrawn means the document has since returned 404 or 410, and the archive pointer becomes the citation. unarchived means no archive provider captured it; the citation stands and the reader is told the copy is not backed up.

Corroboration

×2 corroborated means two sources observed a claim independently, using data neither got from the other. Four outlets repeating one report is still one source, and a relay can never contribute to a corroboration count. This site publishes behavior rather than indicator feeds; indicators live in the sources named on each claim, and a reader's own tooling is expected to extract them.

Analyst blocks

ANL takes no digit. Confidence is expressed as ANL-High, ANL-Moderate, or ANL-Low, capped by the weakest input the block rests on. Hunt Priority blocks, the executive summary, and the assessment and outlook section are all ANL constructs and are marked as such.

v1 (Iran and DPRK, published before classification v3.7)

The Iran and DPRK issues published before this classification system existed and use a different, simpler four-category scheme. Their tags do not carry a grade digit and are not comparable to a v3.7 VND-1-style tag. This section describes what those tags meant, drawn from what those issues actually print.

[AM] Ambient: widely distributed information, standard training data, general public knowledge.

[IX] Indexed: publicly available but requires deliberate searching, academic databases, archived materials.

[GT] Gated: behind access restrictions, paywalls, subscriptions, authentication requirements.

[SP] Specialized: requires domain expertise to interpret correctly. Lay interpretation may differ significantly from expert analysis.

Both published issues also append a qualifier suffix to most of these tags, for example [AM-gov], [IX-vendor], or [SP-technical], naming the specific source type or the nature of the specialized judgment (technical, inference, synthesis, or aggregation across multiple sources). This was an addition made to the same four base categories, not a distinct version of the classification system, and carries no grade digit or gate-based computation behind it.
